What you actually get

Your day at a glance

An estimate that moves, not a morning verdict.

Check your latest energy on iPhone or Apple Watch, and keep it on your watch face with a complication. Follow energy and stress level through the day, touch and hold the timeline to inspect any point, then open any hour to see the activity, stress response and recovery behind its change.

History that explains

See which hours gained or lost energy — and what moved them.

Browse the days saved to your iPhone. Each one breaks down into gained, lost and net energy, an hourly stress strip, and a row per hour naming what shifted it. Stress response estimates how changes in heart rate and heart-rate variability affected your energy; it does not claim to know what caused them.

The longer arc

Compare weeks and months, aligned by time since waking.

Set recent weeks or months against the period before. Comparisons line up by hours since you woke rather than by clock time, so an early morning and a late one still sit on top of each other — and nights you did not wear the watch are not quietly dropped.

Honest by design

Vilar is an estimate, and it presents itself as one. The readings underneath are measured; the energy score is modeled — and the app never blurs the difference.

  • Gaps stay gaps

    Missing measurements remain visible as dashes or gaps. Nothing is filled in with a plausible-looking placeholder and passed off as observed data.

  • It says when it doesn't know

    When there isn't enough information for a reading, Vilar says so, rather than inventing a number to keep the screen full.

  • Your baseline, not a population

    The score is judged against your own recent HRV and resting heart rate. While yours is still forming it starts from population anchors, and the reading says so — labelled Calibrating.

  • No coaching, no verdicts

    Vilar shows you what your day looked like and what moved it. It won't tell you how to train, and it won't grade you.

Privacy Policy — Vilar

Last updated: 30 August 2026

The short version: Vilar has no account and no server. Your health data stays on your Apple Watch and iPhone, and I never see it.

What Vilar reads

With your permission, Vilar reads the following from Apple Health on your Apple Watch:

  • Heart rate variability (HRV)
  • Heart rate and resting heart rate
  • Active energy
  • Steps
  • Sleep
  • Mindful minutes
  • Workouts
  • Body mass — used only to scale the estimate to your body
  • Date of birth and biological sex — used only to estimate your maximum heart rate

Vilar only reads. It never writes anything back to Apple Health. You can grant or revoke any of these at any time in the Health app, under Sharing → Apps.

Where that data goes

  • Your energy estimate is calculated on your Apple Watch.
  • The result syncs to your paired iPhone over Apple's direct watch-to-phone connection, and your day history is stored on that iPhone.
  • Your latest reading is shared with Vilar's own watch complication so it can show a number on your watch face.

That is the entire path, and all of it happens on devices you own. There is no Vilar account, no sign-in, no server, and no cloud service of mine. Vilar contains no networking code at all — it cannot upload your data anywhere, because it has no way to send anything.

What I collect

Nothing. There is no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising identifier, no tracking, and no third-party code of any kind in the app. I cannot see your health data, how you use Vilar, or whether you use it at all.

Your health data is never sold, shared, or used for advertising, marketing, or data mining. It never reaches a third party, because it never leaves your devices.

Your control

  • Revoke Vilar's access to any health category at any time in the Health app. Vilar keeps working with whatever it can still read, and shows the rest as missing rather than guessing.
  • Deleting Vilar from your iPhone deletes the day history stored there. Deleting it from your Apple Watch removes its calculated state.
  • One thing worth knowing: if you back up your iPhone to iCloud or to a computer, Vilar's stored history is included in that backup, the same as any other app's data. That backup is Apple's, covered by Apple's privacy policy and your backup settings — not something Vilar controls.

Because your data never reaches me, there is nothing for me to hand over, export, or delete on request, and nothing that could be exposed if my systems were ever compromised. Your copy is the only copy.

Not a medical service

Vilar is a rough estimate, not a medical device, and the readings it shows are not medical records held by anyone but you.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it, and the current version is always the one published here.
